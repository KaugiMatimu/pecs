21: Course Design & Development Methodology
22: Our tutors /Instructors build every course on a foundation of pre-course research and early input from key stakeholders, so the result is relevant and genuinely owned by everyone involved.
23: The process starts with a training needs analysis looking closely at performance gaps, differences among learners, the role technology plays, and the wider organizational context.
24: From there, the tutor designs the course structure, weighing organizational priorities, existing processes and procedures, budget, available resources, and the nature of what's being taught.
25: Training objectives are then set based on that analysis and design work. Some goals are better served through job aids, on-the-job experience, or equipment changes rather than classroom teaching, so each identified need is examined further to determine the best training approach.
26: Custom training materials and aids built around practical examples, questions, and individual or group exercises are then delivered, either online via webinar or in person at a client-chosen location. Materials are developed against the CIPS curriculum with an emphasis on knowledge retention and workplace application.

49: i. Delivery Strategy
50: Training design and delivery draw on practical adult-learning methods. Experience shows that hands-on modules improve how much trainees retain. A combination of the following methods is typically used:
51: Checklists: Trainees /Apprentices work through a series of questions, answering Yes/No or Agree/Disagree, either individually or in groups depending on the learning objective.
52: Readings: Trainees review short materials on a given topic, written at a level everyone can follow, designed to prompt critical thinking about current or ideal procurement practice.
53: Exercises: Short, targeted exercises done individually or in small groups help trainees apply concepts and connect them to real work.
54: Case studies: An interactive method that keeps trainees engaged; combined with group discussion, it lets more experienced trainees pass on knowledge to the rest of the group.
55: Brainstorming: Used in plenary sessions to work through areas of confusion via open Q&A, weighing the pros and cons of each option before settling on the best one.
56: Role-play: Trainees may be asked to summarise a session or make a short presentation on a topic, building presentation skills alongside subject knowledge.
57: Presentations: Materials built as slide decks and Word documents support instructor-led sessions delivered engagingly, so trainees can compare best practice with what happens back at their own organizations.
58: Storytelling: Trainers share real examples of good and bad practice, and trainees are invited to contribute their own local examples to enrich the discussion.
59: ii. Training Plan
60: The training plan sets out how a program will run, covering location, duration, and number of trainees, along with a time estimate and a list of the materials and methods needed.
61: iii. Training Content

95: CIPS — Global Qualifications
96: CIPS qualifications set the benchmark for professional education in procurement and supply worldwide. CIPS supports its members' professional growth and helps the wider business community raise standards in procurement. Its Diploma in Purchasing and Supply is a high-level qualification, accredited by the UK's Office for Qualifications and Examinations Regulation (Ofqual) and listed on the Register of Regulated Qualifications (register.ofqual.gov.uk).
97: Support is available for each of the following qualification levels ahead of upcoming CIPS exams:
98: CIPS Level 2 Certificate in Procurement and Supply Operations
99: The first of CIPS' three entry-level qualifications. * Suited to those starting in procurement and supply, those for whom it's part of a broader role, or anyone with limited business background looking to move into the field. It's also useful for managers in other disciplines, business leaders, or entrepreneurs who want to understand how procurement works and how it affects business performance. This level covers five core units.
100: *Levels refer to the UK Regulated Qualifications Framework; other regulators may classify levels differently.
101: CIPS Level 3 Advanced Certificate in Procurement and Supply Operations
102: The second entry-level qualification, preparing learners for a procurement and supply career and for progression to the Level 4 Diploma. It comprises four core modules plus one elective.
103: CIPS Level 4 Diploma in Procurement and Supply
104: The third entry-level qualification, made up of eight core modules built around the operational and managerial tiers of the CIPS Global Standard. Candidates begin with L4M1 (Scope and Influence of Procurement and Supply) and finish with L4M8 (Procurement and Supply in Practice); the remaining modules can be taken in any order.
105: CIPS Level 5 Advanced Diploma in Procurement and Supply
106: Built around the managerial and professional tiers of the CIPS Global Standard, this level helps build confidence, strengthen the procurement function's output, and support organizational goals. Candidates complete five core modules plus three electives.
107: CIPS Level 6 Professional Diploma in Procurement and Supply
108: The route to MCIPS status, building on the knowledge and skills developed at Level 5. Candidates complete four core modules plus three electives.
